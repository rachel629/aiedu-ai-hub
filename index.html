Set this up once
Persistent workspace setup

Before you dig into any specific section, set up a persistent workspace so your AI tools always have context about aiEDU — your programs, frameworks, documents, and brand guidance. This pays off across writing, design, slides, and research.

Claude
Create a Claude Project
  1. Go to claude.ai → click Projects in the left sidebar
  2. Create a project: "aiEDU Work"
  3. Add custom instructions: your role, our mission, programs you work on
  4. Upload key docs: rubrics, frameworks, program overviews, recent reports
  5. Every new chat in this project starts with full context — no re-explaining needed
Gemini
Organize Your Google Drive
  1. Create a shared "AI Context" folder in Google Drive
  2. Drop in key docs: program descriptions, rubrics, frameworks, templates
  3. In any Google Doc, click the Gemini icon to ask questions about your Drive files
  4. In Gemini chat, use "@" to reference specific Drive files directly
  5. Any colleague with Drive access can query the same shared context
NotebookLM
Build a Knowledge Notebook
  1. Go to notebooklm.google.com
  2. Create a notebook per topic area: "AI PD Research," "Partner Landscape"
  3. Add sources: PDFs, Google Docs, website URLs, YouTube videos
  4. Ask questions and get cited answers across all sources at once
  5. Share the notebook with teammates so everyone queries the same library
💡 Which one should I use?

Use Claude Projects for deep writing and analysis tasks where you want nuanced, instruction-following AI. Use Gemini + Google Drive when your documents already live in Drive and you want to work without leaving Google. Use NotebookLM when you're synthesizing many sources — especially PDFs and external research — with citations back to the source.

AI Writing & Documents

Use AI to draft, polish, and transform text — reports, proposals, emails, program overviews. Describe what you need in plain language and iterate from there.

The tools
Choose your writing assistant
Claude and Gemini are aiEDU's primary writing tools. Both understand plain English instructions — try them and see what fits your workflow.
Claude
anthropic.com
Excellent at following nuanced instructions, maintaining a consistent voice, and working through complex multi-part documents. Strong for grant writing and program narratives.
Try: "Write this in a warm but professional tone for a foundation audience."
claude.ai →
Gemini
google.com
Best if you live in Google Workspace. Can read and write directly to Google Docs, pull context from your Drive, and help you edit documents without copy-pasting.
Try opening Gemini directly inside Google Docs via the "Help me write" button.
gemini.google.com →
💡 The key habit: start rough, refine fast

You don't need to craft a perfect prompt. Paste your rough notes and say "Turn this into a polished program overview" — then give feedback on the draft. "Make the tone warmer." "Add a section on outcomes." "Cut this down to one page." That back-and-forth is faster than writing from scratch.

💬 Example prompts for aiEDU work
"Here are my rough notes. Turn them into a one-page program overview for school district partners."
"Draft a 2-paragraph executive summary of this report for a funder audience. Keep it under 200 words."
"Rewrite this email so it's warmer and gets to the ask in the first sentence."
"Write a logic model narrative for a program that [describe program]. Include inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes."
"Here is last year's grant report. Help me write this year's based on the same structure but with updated outcomes."
Tips
Get better results
The same content needs different framing for a funder vs. a district partner vs. internal staff. Always tell the AI who will read it: "This is for a foundation that prioritizes equity" or "This is an internal brief for the program team."
You don't need to describe everything from scratch. Paste your rough notes, a previous report, bullet points from a meeting — then ask the AI to transform it. "Clean up these notes into a structured summary" or "Turn these bullets into flowing paragraphs."
If the first draft isn't right, don't rewrite it yourself — ask for variations. "Give me three versions of this opener with different tones." "Write this as a shorter version and a longer version." Pick the best parts and ask the AI to combine them.

Meeting Notes & Follow-ups

Let AI capture and organize your meetings automatically — so you can be present instead of typing. aiEDU has a team license for Granola.

✅ aiEDU has a team license for Granola

You don't need to sign up or pay — ask Rachel or your manager for access. Granola runs quietly in the background during any meeting (Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, in-person) and generates structured notes automatically.

The tools
Meeting note tools
Granola is the primary tool for the team — it runs automatically in the background. Claude or Gemini handle the manual fallback when Granola isn't available.
Granola ⭐
Team licensed
Runs silently in the background on your Mac. Captures system audio from any meeting without a bot joining the call. Generates structured notes with action items. You can add your own shorthand during the meeting and Granola weaves it in.
Download the Mac app at granola.so and sign in with your aiEDU Google account.
granola.so →
Claude / Gemini
Manual option
If you have rough notes or a transcript already, paste them into Claude or Gemini and ask it to clean them up. Great for converting messy bullet points or voice memo transcripts into a structured summary you can send to the team.
Works with any notes — paste them in and ask for a structured summary with decisions, action items, and open questions.
💬 What to ask after a meeting
"Summarize this meeting transcript into: key decisions, action items with owners, and open questions."
"Write a follow-up email to all attendees based on these notes. Include a summary and next steps."
"Pull out every action item from these notes and format as a table: task, owner, due date."
"What topics came up repeatedly across these three meeting notes?" [paste multiple transcripts]
Tips
Make the most of AI meeting notes
Download the Mac app, sign in with your aiEDU Google account, and give it microphone + system audio permissions. It will then capture all your meetings automatically — no setup per-meeting needed. Test it on a short internal call first.
Granola lets you type quick notes while it's recording. You don't need full sentences — just type "follow up w/ Maria on budget" or "!! Rachel to share draft by Friday." Granola weaves your shorthand into the final structured notes intelligently.
Don't just read the auto-generated notes — use them as input for follow-up work. Paste the notes into Claude and ask it to: write the follow-up email, draft a project brief based on what was discussed, or create a one-pager summarizing the decisions made.

Design & Presentations

Create polished decks, graphics, and visual content faster than ever — AI handles the layout, imagery, and structure while you focus on the message.

The tools
Design & presentation tools
Canva is the primary design tool for aiEDU, and Core Design is the right starting point for brand guidance. Use Claude or Gemini to shape the story before you create slides or visuals.
Canva ⭐
Enterprise account
aiEDU has an enterprise Canva account — you already have access. Use Magic Design to generate full branded decks from a text prompt, create AI-generated images, resize for any format, and edit with your brand kit baked in.
Log in with your aiEDU Google account. Try "Magic Design" — paste your content and Canva generates a full branded deck in seconds.
canva.com →
Core Design
aiEDU brand system
aiEDU's internal design system and brand guidelines. Start here to understand the correct colors, fonts, logo usage, and design patterns before creating any public-facing materials in Canva or otherwise.
Check with your manager or the design team for access to the latest Core Design assets and guidelines.
Claude / Gemini
For slide content
Before you open a design tool, use Claude or Gemini to plan your narrative. Ask it to outline a deck, write key messages per slide, or draft speaker notes. Drop the content into Canva once the story is solid.
"Create a 10-slide outline for a funder presentation about our AI literacy program. Each slide: a headline and 3 bullet points."
💬 Example prompts
"Create a 12-slide presentation deck outline for a school district partner about our professional learning offerings."
"Generate a hero image for a slide about AI literacy for educators. Style: clean, professional, optimistic."
"Rewrite these slide bullets so each one is a single punchy sentence, not a fragment."
"Take this wall of text and turn it into 4 slides with clear headings and supporting bullets."
Tips
Work faster with AI design tools
The best presentations start with a clear story, not a beautiful template. Use Claude or Gemini to outline your narrative and key messages before you open any design tool. "What's the one thing I want the audience to remember from this presentation?" is a great prompt to start with.
Instead of searching stock photo sites, use Canva's AI image generator to create visuals that match your exact concept. Be specific: "An illustration of a teacher and student looking at a tablet together, warm colors, flat design style."
Once you have a deck, paste the content into Claude and ask it to turn it into: a one-page leave-behind, a 3-paragraph email summary, social media posts, or a talking points sheet for a phone call.

Research & Synthesis

Upload documents, paste reports, or point AI at a topic — and get instant summaries, comparisons, and insights without reading every page.

The tools
Research & synthesis tools
These three tools let you build persistent context around your work — so you're not re-uploading documents every session.
Claude Projects
anthropic.com
Go beyond one-off document uploads. Claude Projects give you a persistent workspace with custom instructions and uploaded files — so every conversation starts knowing your context. Upload your org's key frameworks, rubrics, or reports once; they stay available across all chats in that project.
Create a project per research area (e.g. "AI PD Research") with relevant docs uploaded. Then ask questions across all of them in one conversation.
claude.ai →
Gemini + Google Drive
google.com
Gemini reads directly from your Google Drive — summarize a Doc, analyze a Sheet, or ask questions across multiple Drive files without downloading anything. The Gemini panel built into Google Docs lets you query or rewrite any document without leaving the page.
In Google Docs, click the Gemini icon (or "Help me write") to summarize the doc, pull key themes, or suggest edits — no copy-pasting.
gemini.google.com →
NotebookLM
Google — free
Upload PDFs, Google Docs, websites, or YouTube videos. NotebookLM builds a private knowledge base and lets you ask questions across all sources with citations back to the original. Can also generate a surprisingly good AI "podcast" summary of your sources.
Best for synthesizing 5–10 research papers at once — it will cite exactly where in the source each answer comes from.
notebooklm.google.com →
🤔 Gemini or NotebookLM — which one?

Use Gemini when your sources are already in Google Drive, you want to work inside Google Docs or Sheets, or you need quick answers from a handful of documents you already have. Use NotebookLM when you're pulling together sources from multiple places (PDFs, websites, YouTube), want to build a growing knowledge base over time, or need citations that point back to the exact sentence in a source.

💬 Example prompts
"Summarize this report in 3 bullet points. Then give me 5 key findings most relevant to K-12 AI education."
"I've uploaded 4 research papers. What do they agree on? Where do they disagree?"
"What are the most important themes across these stakeholder interview transcripts?"
"What does recent research say about effective professional development models for AI literacy? Cite sources."
Tips
Get more from research tools
Don't try to summarize a report yourself and then ask AI to help — just upload the PDF. Claude and NotebookLM can process hundreds of pages instantly. The more raw material you give them, the better the output.
Don't just ask "summarize this." Specify: "Give me a 1-page executive summary," "Create a table of key findings," "Write 3 paragraphs I can include in a grant proposal," or "List the top 5 takeaways as bullet points with a sentence of context each."
Create a notebook for each major research area (e.g., "AI PD effectiveness research," "State AI policy landscape"). Add sources as you find them. Over time it becomes a searchable knowledge base you can query anytime — and share with colleagues.

Workflows

Build workflows that connect your tools and automate repetitive tasks — from routing meeting summaries to Slack, to running multi-step AI pipelines.

The tools
Workflow tools
These platforms let you build multi-step workflows that connect your tools — no code required for the basics.
Make
make.com
A visual workflow builder where you connect apps with drag-and-drop. Each "scenario" runs automatically when triggered. Make has deep Claude/OpenAI integration — you can add an AI step anywhere in a workflow to generate, classify, or transform content.
Good first automation: new Granola meeting note → Claude summarizes → posts to Slack channel.
make.com →
Zapier
zapier.com
The most widely used automation tool, with 6,000+ app integrations. Simpler and more guided than Make. Good for straightforward trigger → action flows. Zapier also has a built-in AI step that uses ChatGPT to generate or transform text.
Easier to start with than Make. Great for simple two-step automations like form submission → draft email.
zapier.com →
Claude Projects
Built-in context
Claude's Projects feature lets you create a persistent AI assistant with custom instructions and uploaded documents. Not a full automation platform, but great for creating specialized assistants — like a grant-writing helper that always knows your org's context and voice.
"You are an expert grant writer for aiEDU, an education nonprofit focused on AI literacy. Here is our mission, theory of change, and recent program data..."
claude.ai →
🔗 Where to start: one simple workflow

Pick one task you do manually at least weekly and automate just that. Good candidates: copy Granola notes to a shared doc, get a Slack message when a new form is submitted, auto-draft a follow-up email template when a meeting ends. Start small, learn the platform, then build up.

💬 Workflow ideas for aiEDU work
New meeting note in Granola → AI summarizes → posts digest to #team-updates Slack channel
Google Form submission → AI generates a personalized response draft → sends to your inbox for review
New research paper added to a shared Google Drive folder → NotebookLM summary → posted to team doc
Calendar event ends → AI drafts follow-up email → saves to Drafts for you to review and send
Tips
Build workflows that actually stick
Before opening Make or Zapier, write out every step you do manually. "I get a form submission → I copy it to a spreadsheet → I draft an email response → I send it." Automation platforms mirror this structure — each step becomes a module in your workflow.
Describe what you want to automate to Claude and ask it to outline the workflow steps, suggest which apps to connect, and write any AI prompt that should be part of the automation. "I want to automatically summarize new emails from grant contacts and post them to Slack. What would the Make scenario look like?"
Until you fully trust an automation, build in a human review step — auto-generate the draft, but don't auto-send it. Have Make post a draft to a Google Doc or save it to Gmail Drafts so you can review before it goes out. Remove the human step once you've confirmed the quality is reliable.

Data & Analysis

Pull data from Airtable, analyze it in plain English, and turn it into charts and summaries — no SQL or coding required.

The approach
AI as your data analyst
Most of aiEDU's program data lives in Airtable. These workflows let you move that data into an AI tool and ask questions, create visualizations, and generate summaries — without writing a single formula.
Claude
anthropic.com
Export a CSV from Airtable and upload it directly to Claude. Ask questions in plain English: "What are the top 5 districts by participant count?" or "Summarize trends across cohorts." Claude can also write formulas, suggest visualizations, and draft narrative summaries from your data.
Best for: narrative summaries, pattern analysis, and generating charts as code (HTML/JS) you can paste anywhere.
claude.ai →
Gemini + Google Sheets
google.com
Export Airtable data to Google Sheets, then use the Gemini panel in Sheets to ask questions, write formulas, and create charts. The Gemini sidebar in Sheets lets you describe what you want and it builds the formula or chart for you.
In Google Sheets: click the Gemini icon in the sidebar → ask "Create a bar chart of participant counts by program" and it builds it automatically.
sheets.google.com →
Airtable + Claude
Connected workflow
For recurring reports, set up a Make or Zapier workflow that exports Airtable data on a schedule and sends it to Claude for a summary. The summary can be posted to Slack or saved to a Google Doc automatically.
Good for: weekly program reports, cohort progress snapshots, or stakeholder updates that repeat on a schedule.
📋 Getting data out of Airtable

In any Airtable view, click the grid icon → Download CSV to export your current filtered view. Then upload the CSV to Claude or import it into Google Sheets. For Airtable connections to apps you're building, check with Kate before connecting to production bases.

💬 Example prompts
"Here's a CSV of our program participants. How many are from each district? Show as a table."
"What trends do you see in survey response scores across these three cohorts?"
"Write a 3-paragraph narrative summary of this data for a funder report."
"Create a formula in Google Sheets that calculates the completion rate for each row."
"Here are our quarterly impact numbers. Create an HTML bar chart I can paste into a document."
Tips
Get better results from data analysis
AI works best with clean, consistent data. Before uploading a CSV, check for: blank rows, inconsistent capitalization (e.g. "Ohio" vs "ohio"), merged cells, or columns with mixed data types. A few minutes of cleanup saves a lot of confusion in the analysis.
When you upload data, give context: "This is a survey of K-12 educators who attended our PD sessions. Each row is one respondent. The 'score' column is their self-reported AI confidence on a 1–5 scale." The more the AI understands the meaning behind the columns, the more useful the analysis.
Before uploading any Airtable export, check whether it contains personally identifiable information — names, emails, phone numbers. If so, either anonymize the data first or aggregate it to remove individual rows. When in doubt, check with Kate or your manager before uploading participant data to any AI tool.

Vibe Coding

Prototype real tools quickly by describing what you want in plain English. For aiEDU, the recommended path is simple: start small, keep it lightweight, and only add complexity if the prototype proves useful.

The big idea
🤔
Start with a prototype, not a perfect build

Vibe coding is a fast way to turn an idea into a working tool. You describe the experience in plain English, and the AI helps you build a first version you can test with real users.

For aiEDU, the best path is usually a lightweight prototype that can be shared internally or with partners quickly — then refined based on feedback.

Recommended path
Start with one of these four tools

These are the options we recommend for aiEDU staff. Pick the one that matches your comfort level and the kind of prototype you want to make.

Lovable

app-builder · lovable.dev
Great for polished, shareable web apps with little setup. Best when you want an attractive first version quickly.
  • Fast for polished prototypes
  • No coding required
  • Built-in hosting
lovable.dev →

Bolt.new

app-builder · bolt.new
Best for prototypes that may need more logic or a data-backed workflow. Good if you want to move from a mockup to something more functional.
  • Good for more complex builds
  • Works in the browser
  • Fast iteration
bolt.new →

Claude

chat-to-code · claude.ai
Fastest way to get a working prototype in chat. Great for single-page tools, calculators, or quick interactive experiences.
  • Zero setup
  • Great for fast drafts
  • Easy to share
claude.ai →

AI Studio

prototyping · aistudio.google.com
A strong option when you want Gemini's capabilities built directly into the experience, especially for forms, quizzes, or AI-assisted tools.
  • Gemini built in
  • Good for AI-first prototypes
  • Easy to share
aistudio.google.com →
💡 Recommended workflow

Start with Claude or Lovable for a quick prototype. If you need more structure or a more functional build, move to Bolt.new. Use AI Studio when Gemini is the better fit for the experience you want to create.

💬 Good first prompts
"Build a one-page internal tool for staff to review AI readiness self-assessment responses."
"Create a simple quiz experience for teachers that gives instant feedback and a summary at the end."
"Make a lightweight resource hub with a search bar and featured program cards."
Good first projects
What is worth prototyping first?

These are the kinds of tools that usually benefit from a fast first version before you decide whether they need more complexity.

🧠 Internal helpers

Simple calculators, checklists, resource finders, or decision-support tools.

📚 Interactive resources

Quizzes, guided reflection tools, or mini-learning experiences for partners and staff.

⚙️ Lightweight workflows

A simple form that collects input and turns it into a structured summary or next step.
